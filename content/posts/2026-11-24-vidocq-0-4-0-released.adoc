Vidocq 0.4.0 is on Maven Central. Two release candidates, eighteen days of your reports and ours, and the line is
final: no `-RC` any more, the installer picks it up, and the documentation's default version is 0.4.0.

It is also the first release cut from *one repository*: more on that <<monorepo,below>>.

If you followed the release candidates, here is what 0.4.0 adds on top of them:

* *Your data, seen from the inside.* Jakarta Persistence 3.2 runs on Vidocq, and `vidocq dev` gives it a panel that
  draws your model, runs your JPQL and shows the SQL it would create.
* *The Web Profile fills in, as a preview.* WebSockets, the Standard Tag Library and Jakarta Security join Servlet,
  Pages and Expression Language.
* *A dev loop that forgets nothing.* A change in a method body is swapped into the running JVM: the beans, the HTTP
  sessions and the open pages keep their state.
* *Less to write, less to load.* A project no longer needs our parent pom, configuration profiles work, and the start
  replays a record of the build instead of doing the work again.

The complete list is in the https://doc.vidocq.dev/vidocq/0.4.0/whats-new[What's new of the runtime] and in the
https://doc.vidocq.dev/home/whats-new[What's new of the ecosystem], which links each brick's own page. Each of those
pages has three sections: what 0.4.0 added since RC2, what RC2 added, and what RC1 brought since 0.3.0. If you come
from 0.3.0, read all three, starting with the link:/posts/vidocq-0-4-0-rc2-is-available-and-it-s-fast/[RC2 post].

[#monorepo]
== One repository, one version

Up to RC2, Vidocq was twenty repositories: one per brick, one for the parent, one for the runtime. Releasing meant
tagging eighteen of them in the right order, and a change to Vauban, our CDI container, meant a branch in Vauban, then
a companion branch in every brick that used the changed API, each waiting for the snapshot of the one before.

Since 20 November, every component of the release, the runtime, its Maven plugin and its CLI live in one repository,
https://codefloe.com/Vidocq/vidocq[Vidocq/vidocq], built as one Maven reactor under one version. For you, it changes
little: the artifacts keep their coordinates, and the BOM still aligns them. For us, and for anyone who contributes,
it changes a lot:

* *One pull request for one change.* A change to a shared API fixes every caller in the same pull request, and the
  build of that pull request rebuilds and tests every module downstream of what changed.
* *One version, one release.* 0.4.0 is one tag, `v0.4.0`, and every artifact of it was built from the same commit.
* *The maturity of a component is where it lives.* `vidocq-components/` holds the stable bricks, `vidocq-preview/` the
  usable ones that are not complete or not certified yet (Noether, Kerckhoffs), and `vidocq-incubator/` what is built
  and tested but never released. Moving up a level is one commit.
* *Third-party integrations live next door.* Extensions that depend on other libraries, such as the Vaadin and
  LangChain4j ones, moved to their own organisation,
  https://codefloe.com/VidocqCodeUniverse_VCU[VidocqCodeUniverse_VCU], where third-party dependencies are allowed and
  the Vidocq repository keeps its zero-dependency rule. More extensions will follow them there. Their Maven
  coordinates do not change: the same `groupId`, `artifactId` and version, so your pom keeps working as it is.

[#persistence]
== Your data, seen from the inside

image::/images/posts/2026-11-24-persistence.jpeg["Viduke studies a map of linked boxes pinned on a wall in an attic under the Paris roofs, a lantern in his hand, teal threads joining the boxes"]

https://doc.vidocq.dev/noether/0.4.0/[Noether], our Jakarta Persistence 3.2 implementation, is now a Vidocq extension.
One command wires it, with the Mansart pool and Mansart Transactions it runs on:

[source,bash]
----
vidocq extension add noether-persistence
----

Your `persistence.xml` units run on the application's pools. `@PersistenceContext` and `@PersistenceUnit` are
injected, and so is plain `@Inject`: every unit is an `EntityManagerFactory` bean named after it, a JTA unit also
gives a transaction-scoped `EntityManager`, and its `CriteriaBuilder`, `Metamodel` or `SchemaManager` are one
`@Inject` away, under the qualifier and the scope your `<qualifier>` and `<scope>` elements name, as the Jakarta EE 11
platform asks. A JTA unit and a Mansart Data repository on the same pool share the connection of a transaction: each
sees what the other wrote, before the commit.

No `persistence.xml`? The `vidocq.persistence.<unit>.*` keys declare a unit with its module, packages or classes. A
library module may carry its own `persistence.xml` next to its entities. And a component written for a Jakarta EE
server finds its pools where it expects them: `java:comp/DefaultDataSource`, `java:comp/env/<name>`.

Then start `vidocq dev` and open the dev console. The *Persistence* panel has a unit selector above six tabs:

* the units, their entities and their pool;
* a *graph of your model*, entities as boxes, relations as lines, a superclass above its subclasses;
* a *JPQL editor* for queries and writes, rolled back unless you ask it to commit;
* a paged view of an entity, whose selected rows open a ready-to-run `DELETE` or `UPDATE`;
* the indexes you declared against the ones the database has;
* the create and drop scripts, with a *Download* button.

The injected `EntityManager` goes through a client proxy generated when the extension is built, not through a
`java.lang.reflect.Proxy`: no reflection on the path of each call.

Noether is a *preview*: the Persistence TCK passes its non-skipped tests on PostgreSQL with a local patch, and we
claim no certification. The https://doc.vidocq.dev/noether/0.4.0/tck[TCK page] says exactly what runs, and the
https://doc.vidocq.dev/noether/0.4.0/getting-started[getting-started page] takes you from an entity to a query. To see
it all at once, `vidocq-runtime-noether-persistence-example` is a small reading notebook: three related entities, one
JTA unit, a REST resource.

[#web-profile]
== The Web Profile fills in, as a preview

image::/images/posts/2026-11-24-web.jpeg["Viduke sends a beam of teal light from one Paris rooftop to another, where a small lit window answers him, hexadecimal digits floating along the beam"]

Vidocq's home ground is the Core Profile and MicroProfile. Its Jakarta EE Web Profile side is a *preview* in 0.4.0:
usable, released, with official TCK runs behind each brick, but not complete and not certified. Try it, build on it,
tell us what breaks; do not bet a production system on it yet. Three Jakarta specifications join Servlet (Foy), Pages
(Ibarra) and Expression Language (Peano):

https://doc.vidocq.dev/foy/0.4.0/whats-new[Jakarta WebSocket 2.2]::
`vidocq-runtime-foy-websocket-extension` brings Foy's WebSocket server API to the application. A Vaadin application
gets real push over WebSocket through the
https://codefloe.com/VidocqCodeUniverse_VCU/vidocq_vcu_extension_vaadin[Vaadin extension]. One key,
`vidocq.websocket.allowed-origins`, lists the origins allowed to open a WebSocket besides your own, for Jakarta
WebSocket endpoints and native Chappe mounts alike; a refused origin gets a `403` and a warning that names the key. At
stop, every open WebSocket gets a proper `1001` Close.

https://doc.vidocq.dev/bodoni/0.4.0/[Jakarta Standard Tag Library 3.0]::
Bodoni gives the pages Ibarra compiles the `c:`, `fmt:`, `fn:`, `x:` and `sql:` tags. The official Tags TCK passes
541 of 541 tests on Bodoni, Ibarra, Foy and Peano together (a TCK run, not a certification).
`vidocq extension add bodoni-jstl` wires it.

https://doc.vidocq.dev/kerckhoffs/0.4.0/[Jakarta Security 4.0]::
Kerckhoffs, in preview, puts your authentication under Jakarta Security, on the Servlet extension: identity stores,
the BASIC and FORM mechanisms, your own mechanism, `SecurityContext.authenticate` and `@RememberMe`. The official
Security TCK on the assembled runtime passes its default set, 81 tests. `vidocq extension add kerckhoffs-security`.

And a bean can now `@Inject` the `HttpServletRequest`, the `HttpSession` and the `ServletContext`, as on any Jakarta EE
server. A Jakarta REST application can also run as a plain servlet, through the new `cassini-servlet` module.

[#dev-loop]
== A dev loop that forgets nothing

image::/images/posts/2026-11-24-dev.jpeg["Viduke swaps a glowing gear in a large clock on a Paris rooftop at night while the clock keeps running, its hands still turning"]

RC2 made `vidocq dev` fast. 0.4.0 makes it gentle with your state.

*Hot swap.* When you change only method bodies, `vidocq dev` no longer restarts anything: the JVM redefines the
changed classes in place, and your beans, your HTTP sessions and your open pages keep their state. The log says so:

----
Hot reload - redefined 2 classes in 14 ms, the application keeps running
----

*Sessions that survive a bigger change.* When a change does need a new application layer, the servlet deployment
writes the HTTP sessions out at the stop and reads them back before it serves again: a Vaadin page keeps its UI, a
shopping cart keeps its items. When a session cannot come back, because a serializable class lost a field for
instance, `vidocq dev` drops it and says which class and why.

*It tells you why it reloads.* After each recompile, `vidocq dev` compares what was compiled with what runs. An
edited comment reloads nothing; any other change names its reason, such as
`Hot reload - restarting on a fresh application layer: MainView gained a method lambda$new$1`.

*A save seen sooner on macOS.* The JDK's file watcher on macOS looks every two seconds. `vidocq dev` now reads the
file times itself, every 200 ms: from a save to its detection, the median went from 908 ms to 349 ms on our
measurement. Linux keeps the JDK's watcher, which listens to the kernel there instead of polling.

image::/images/posts/2026-11-24-save-seen.svg["From a save to its detection under vidocq dev on macOS, median of 10 saves: 908 ms with the JDK's file watcher, 349 ms with the file times read every 200 ms"]

*Tests that point at the line.* A failed test now prints where it failed, `(OrderServiceTest.java:31)`; that was
lost for tests running in a named module. A test run gets dev services of its own, so a test's `TRUNCATE` never
empties the database you are clicking through. And a second `vidocq dev` on the same machine starts: its debug port
moves aside, with a warning that says so.

All of it is in the https://doc.vidocq.dev/vidocq/0.4.0/continuous-testing[continuous testing] and
https://doc.vidocq.dev/vidocq/0.4.0/dev-console[dev console] pages.

[#less]
== Less to write, less to load

*No parent pom required.* `vidocq create` now writes a pom that imports the BOM and declares the Vidocq plugin as a
build extension. The plugin gives the build what our parent used to carry, so your company parent stays where it is.
`--with-parent` still writes the former style, and two commands move an existing project:

[source,bash]
----
vidocq pom convert --to parentless   # or --to parent; shows the diff first, --check for CI
vidocq pom upgrade --release         # moves the pom to the latest Vidocq, either style
----

*Configuration that behaves like MicroProfile Config.* `%dev.vidocq.http.port=8081` now applies under `vidocq dev`, or
wherever `vidocq.profile` names the profile, and `${DASHBOARD_PORT:8080}` is expanded in Vidocq's own keys, with or
without Ravel. `vidocq doctor` reads the descriptions of the configuration keys your jars ship and warns about a value
that does not fit.

*A start that replays the build.* CDI build compatible extensions are a great way to compute at build time, but a
container still ran them at every start. Vidocq now runs them once, in `vidocq:generate`, and freezes what they did;
the start loads that record. On an application with ten such extensions (REST, Config, Health, Metrics, OpenAPI, JWT,
REST Client, Fault Tolerance, Telemetry, Transactions), the median start went from 430 ms to 391 ms, and the CDI build
phase from 227 ms to 172 ms. The startup report says `cdi extensions  10 extensions frozen at build`.

image::/images/posts/2026-11-24-frozen-start.svg["Start of an application with ten build compatible extensions, median of 15 starts: 430 ms to Started when they run at start, 391 ms when frozen at build; CDI build phase 227 ms against 172 ms"]

We also chased the classes a start loads. The features added since late October had cost a start about 400 classes, many of
them lambdas spun on the start path. Rewriting those loops gave 270 of them back, and the memory at rest of our bench
application went from 149 MB to 115 MB with them.

If you missed the headline numbers of RC2, they still describe this line: one small REST application served
80,000 requests per second with a p99 of 8.8 ms, started in 0.87 seconds and held 127 MB at rest, on a 2018 Mac mini.
They were measured on the 0.4.0 line just before RC2, and the link:/posts/vidocq-0-4-0-rc2-is-available-and-it-s-fast/[RC2 post] says
how. We will measure 0.4.0 itself with the next round of our benchmark suites.

[#production]
== Shipping and running, one step further

* *Two ways to ship a container.* `vidocq:docker` still builds a jlink image by default. With
  `-Dvidocq.docker.kind=jdk`, it copies the distribution onto an Eclipse Temurin JRE instead, so an encrypted jar or a
  library that is not a Java module works as it is. The new
  https://doc.vidocq.dev/vidocq/0.4.0/shipping[Choosing how to ship] page says which form fits which case.
* *A jlink image is one plain modular application*, one copy of each module, by construction.
* *Transactions you can trust under failure.* A two-phase commit reaches every resource even when one fails, the
  transaction timeout is applied, an `Error` rolls back, transaction ids carry a node id, and the recovery journal is
  compacted. The pool validates idle connections, keeps `minIdle`, pools XA connections and resets what a borrower
  changed before the next one gets the connection.
* *Safer answers.* An exception no `ExceptionMapper` handles answers a bare `500`: its message, which may carry SQL or
  request values, stays in the log. A JWT configuration that would fetch keys over cleartext HTTP from a remote host
  stops the start.

[#upgrading]
== Upgrading

[source,bash]
----
vidocq update --release        # the CLI moves to 0.4.0, whatever channel you installed
vidocq pom upgrade --release   # your pom moves to 0.4.0
vidocq doctor                  # checks the project; --fix makes the changes it can
----

From RC2, three things deserve a look, each with its note on the
https://doc.vidocq.dev/vidocq/0.4.0/migration[migration page]:

* *The application layer holds your libraries by default* (`vidocq.app.layer=all`). An application that worked keeps
  working: where `all` cannot apply, the build warns and falls back.
* *Native WebSocket mounts refuse another origin* with a `403`. List the origins you trust in
  `vidocq.websocket.allowed-origins`.
* *Mansart Data no longer quotes table and column names*, as Jakarta Persistence does not: the database folds them.
  A name that relied on its exact case needs its quotes in the mapping.

== Try it

[source,bash]
----
curl -fsSL https://vidocq.dev/install.sh | sh

[#how-we-measured]
== How we measured

* *Frozen extensions.* Vauban `BENCH-20261113-01`: an application made with `vidocq create` and ten extensions,
  packaged with and without the record, 15 cold starts per series and three rounds, on an Apple M5 Max with Temurin
  25. The figures above are the quiet series, medians of the three rounds' medians; a second series on a busier
  machine gave 500 to 561 ms against 463 to 480 ms.
* *Classes at start.* Vauban `BENCH-20261118-01`: the Cassini application of our HTTP bench, 9 starts per
  distribution, classes counted by the JVM's own counters, on the same machine under load. The class counts do not
  depend on the load; the times did, so we do not quote them.
* *Save detection.* Runtime `BENCH.md`, 2026-11-18: ten saves of a method body in a Cassini example under
  `vidocq dev`, the JDK's watcher against the 200 ms reading, on the same machine.
* *RC2 figures.* Measured on the 0.4.0 line before RC2, as the RC2 post explains; 0.4.0 itself is not measured yet.
* Every figure has its entry, with the exact command and the raw results, in the `BENCH.md` of its component in
  https://codefloe.com/Vidocq/vidocq[Vidocq/vidocq].
