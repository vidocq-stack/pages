Vidocq 0.4.0-RC2 is on Maven Central. Eleven days after RC1, the whole family went out again: *eighteen
repositories* tagged `v0.4.0-RC2`, from `vidocq-parent` to the runtime and its CLI, with about 1,050 commits since
`v0.4.0-RC1`. It is still a release candidate, and we want you to try it before `0.4.0`.

Three things to know about it:

* *It is fast.* On a 2018 Mac mini, one small REST application on Vidocq serves *80,000 requests per second* with a
  p99 latency of *8.8 ms*, *starts in 0.87 seconds* and holds *127 MB* at rest. The whole application weighs *2.8 MB*.
* *It restores from a snapshot.* With CRaC, a warmed-up Vidocq application comes back in a fresh container in about
  *400 ms*, whatever its start-up took.
* *It is a joy to work with.* `vidocq dev` starts your database, reloads in about two seconds, runs your tests and
  names the one that fails; `vidocq doctor` checks the project; `vidocq build docker` works on macOS, Linux and Windows.
  And the `opens` lines are not your job any more: in the session below, you write none.

The full list is in the https://doc.vidocq.dev/vidocq/0.4.0-RC2/whats-new[What's new of the runtime] and in the
https://doc.vidocq.dev/home/whats-new[What's new of the ecosystem], which links each brick's own page.

[#fast]
== It's fast

The numbers below are Vidocq's own, and nothing else: no ranking, no other framework, only what one small REST
application on Vidocq does on one machine. And not a big machine: a *Mac mini from 2018*, the one with the Intel Core
i7-8700B, turned into a Linux bench box, turbo off. The application got four of its six cores at most.

The JVM is Eclipse Temurin 25 with the G1 collector and a fixed 1 GB heap. That is all: no native image, no AOT
cache, no CRaC, no tuning flag. The application is a Todo API written the way you would write it: a Jakarta REST
resource, a CDI bean, JSON both ways, 1,000 todos kept in memory.

=== 80,000 requests per second

The load comes from https://github.com/giltene/wrk2[wrk2], on 2 cores of its own, with 100 connections. wrk2 sends
requests at a *constant rate* and measures latency from the moment each request should have been sent, so a server
that falls behind cannot hide it. The mix looks like a real Todo client: 65 % reads of one todo, 20 % creations,
10 % lists of 100 todos and 5 % updates. Vidocq gets 4 physical cores (8 hardware threads), and we call a rate
*sustained* when at least 98 % of the requests asked are served and the p99 latency stays under 10 ms.

image::/images/posts/2026-11-06-throughput.svg["Requests per second served by Vidocq at each rate asked, with the p99 latency of each: 4,990 at 5,000 asked (p99 1.9 ms), 9,902 at 10,000 (1.9 ms), 19,959 at 20,000 (2.2 ms), 39,918 at 40,000 (2.1 ms), 59,874 at 60,000 (2.6 ms) and 79,833 at 80,000 (8.8 ms)",title="Requests per second served, with the p99 latency of each rate"]

[cols="1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| Requests per second asked | Requests per second served | p99 latency

| 5,000 | 4,990 | 1.93 ms
| 10,000 | 9,902 | 1.91 ms
| 20,000 | 19,959 | 2.18 ms
| 40,000 | 39,918 | 2.08 ms
| 60,000 | 59,874 | 2.56 ms
| 80,000 | 79,833 | 8.81 ms
|===

Every rate is sustained. From 5,000 to 60,000 requests per second, 99 % of the answers come back in about 2 ms, and
the curve is flat: Vidocq barely notices the load. At 80,000 requests per second, the highest rate we asked for, the
p99 rises to 8.8 ms, still under the line. We did not ask for more, so the ceiling is somewhere above.

image::/images/posts/2026-11-06-p99.svg["The p99 latency of Vidocq against the rate asked: about 2 ms from 5,000 to 60,000 requests per second, 8.8 ms at 80,000, under the 10 ms limit for a sustained rate",title="p99 latency against the rate: flat up to 60,000 requests per second"]

Under the hood, things stay calm. At 40,000 requests per second, the server keeps about three of its eight hardware
threads busy. Over the whole life of that container, the garbage collector runs 27 times, and its longest pause is
6.8 ms. Each connection has its own virtual thread and your code stays plain, blocking Java: there is no thread pool
to size and no reactive code to write.

=== Under a second to start

image::/images/posts/2026-11-06-start.jpeg["Viduke bursts off a starting line painted on a Paris rooftop, next to a small aluminium computer, while a pocket watch floating above him has barely moved"]

Start-up is measured from the moment the container starts to the first `200` answer of the health endpoint, as the
median of 7 cold starts, each in a fresh container.

image::/images/posts/2026-11-06-startup.svg["Vidocq start-up time by number of cores, median of 7 cold starts: 1.61 s on 1 core, 0.98 s on 2 cores, 0.87 s on 4 cores",title="Start-up, from the container start to the first answer"]

On 4 cores, Vidocq is up in *0.87 seconds*, and it answers its first real request, the list of 100 todos, in 44 ms.
Even on a single core, it is up in 1.61 seconds. When Vidocq starts there is nothing to discover and nothing to
generate: it loads about 1,900 classes, wires what was already wired, opens its socket and answers.

=== Travelling light

image::/images/posts/2026-11-06-travel-light.jpeg["Viduke stands on a Paris rooftop at night and holds up a tiny open leather case, in which a whole miniature Paris glows, Eiffel Tower included"]

The memory below is the resident memory of the whole process (RSS), on 4 cores, read 10 seconds after the
application is ready, then again after 20 seconds of traffic at 1,000 requests per second.

image::/images/posts/2026-11-06-memory.svg["Resident memory of the Vidocq process on 4 cores: 127 MB at rest, 10 seconds after start, and 283 MB after 20 seconds of traffic",title="Resident memory of the process, at rest and after traffic"]

At rest, Vidocq holds *127 MB*, with 25 threads; after its first 20,000 requests, 283 MB. The heap was set to a full
1 GB from the start: these figures are what the process actually touches, not a limit we squeezed it into. And the
application itself, with all of Vidocq and every Jakarta API jar it runs on, is *2.8 MB* on disk, the JVM aside.

[#less-reflection]
== Why it's fast: even less reflection

None of this comes from a trick in the application. It comes from what Vidocq does before the application runs: the
CDI wiring, the JSON binding, the REST routing and the data access are generated as code at build time, so there is
no reflection and no classpath scanning to pay for at start-up or per request. RC2 pushes that further:

* *JSON-B without reflection for almost everything you exchange.* The types your resources take and return get their
  binding generated at build time: records and final classes, but also plain classes with getters and setters and
  their hierarchies (`Product extends BaseEntity`), date and number formats, arrays, adapters, serializers,
  `@JsonbCreator`, polymorphism over a sealed interface, the entity of a `Response`, and the DTOs of a library.
* *Jakarta Data without `opens`.* Mansart reaches your entities, your repository implementations and your projection
  records through code its processor generates, including for `@Repository` interfaces shipped in a library.
* *Expression Language, Config and the MicroProfile annotations at build time.* Peano generates the accessors of the
  `@Named` beans your pages read, Ravel the factories of your `@ConfigProperties` classes and their converters;
  Fault Tolerance, Metrics and OpenAPI read your annotations from companions written when your code compiles, and the
  JWT extension runs at compile time too.
* *Less work per request in CDI.* Request-scoped and dependent beans, private `@Inject` fields and interceptor
  methods no longer go through reflection, and a bean is read once instead of at each creation.
* *Your dependencies get their code too.* `vidocq:generate` now runs on every build: the jars your application
  depends on get the beans, repositories and bindings their bricks generate for them, with nothing to declare.

And when something still falls back to reflection, Vidocq says so: a class generated at run time is listed at boot,
and a type that keeps the reflective JSON mapping is named in the build output, with the reason. The
https://doc.vidocq.dev/vidocq/0.4.0-RC2/internals[internals page] lists every generator and what it produces.

[#crac]
== And when a second is still too long

If 0.87 seconds is still too slow for you, RC2 supports CRaC. Take a snapshot of a warmed-up application once, and
every new container restores it in about *400 ms*, whatever the start-up took: on an application that needs eight
minutes to warm up, that is 1,300 times faster than a cold start. Your own code takes part through two CDI events,
`Checkpoint` and `Restore`, to let go of a client or a file before the snapshot and take it back after, with no
`jdk.crac` in its code. The whole story, numbers included, is in
link:/posts/checkpoint-restore-answer-vidocq-on-crac/[Checkpoint, Restore, Answer: Vidocq on CRaC], and the how-to
in the https://doc.vidocq.dev/vidocq/0.4.0-RC2/crac[documentation].

Now the code. A migration, an entity, a Jakarta Data repository and a resource, in a new package `todos`:

[source,sql]
----
CREATE TABLE todos (
    id    BIGSERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    title VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    done  BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT FALSE
);
----

[source,java]
----
@Entity
@Table(name = "todos")
public class Todo {

    @Id
    @GeneratedValue
    private Long id;
    private String title;
    private boolean done;

    // getters and setters
}

@Repository
public interface Todos extends BasicRepository<Todo, Long> {

    List<Todo> findByDone(boolean done);
}

@Path("/todos")
@ApplicationScoped
@Produces(MediaType.APPLICATION_JSON)
@Consumes(MediaType.APPLICATION_JSON)
public class TodoResource {

    @Inject
    Todos todos;

    @GET
    public List<Todo> list(@QueryParam("done") Boolean done) {
        return done == null ? todos.findAll().toList() : todos.findByDone(done);
    }

    @POST
    public Todo add(Todo todo) {
        return todos.save(todo);
    }
}
----

=== Tests that say what broke

    @Test

        VidocqResponse created = application.post("/todos", "application/json", "{\"title\":\"Write a test\"}");

=== And the rest of the toolbox

* *`vidocq dev` survives a first build that does not compile*: it reports the error and builds again on your next
  save, instead of exiting.
* *Extensions write their settings.* `vidocq extension add humboldt-telemetry` names the service and points it at a
  local collector; a Grafana dev service (`grafana/otel-lgtm`) shows the traces, and with the pool every SQL statement
  is a span of the request. JWT gets commented hints, and the Keycloak dev service publishes its issuer, token URL and
  admin console.
* *`--help` on every command*, `JAVA_OPTS` honoured by the packaged launchers, and `@VidocqTest` requests that carry
  headers, for a bearer token.
* *`vidocq update`* keeps the CLI itself up to date, and tells you once a day at most when a newer one is out.

[#upgrade]
== Upgrading from RC1

Bump the parent (or the BOM) to `0.4.0-RC2` and run `vidocq doctor`: it reads your project and says what to change,
and `--fix` makes the changes it can. Under `vidocq-runtime-parent` that is usually all. A few things deserve a look:

* *Four more codegen bundles.* Metrics, OpenAPI, Fault Tolerance and JWT now run their processor at build time.
  The parent wires them for you; under your own parent, `vidocq:checkpom` fails the build and prints the `<path>` to
  paste. See the https://doc.vidocq.dev/vidocq/0.4.0-RC2/migration[migration page].
* *A repository method no query derives from fails the build*, instead of throwing at run time.
  `-Amansart.unresolvedMethods=warn` keeps the old behaviour.
* *Mansart Data brings its SQL dialects*: you can drop the one you declared.
* *Behaviour you may notice*: a synchronous `@Timeout` method runs on the caller's thread; a typed REST client call
  throws `WebApplicationException` on an error status; the OpenAPI document no longer lists `/health` and `/metrics`;
  Telemetry exports over OTLP as soon as an endpoint is set.
* *`vidocq:generate` runs on every build.* `-Dvidocq.generate.skip` turns it off.

And three rough edges we found while writing this post, with their workaround until the fixes land:

* Under `vidocq dev`, the first test run of a project whose test dependencies were never downloaded fails to resolve
  them, because it runs Maven offline. Run `vidocq test --once` (or `./mvnw test`) once
  (https://codefloe.com/Vidocq/vidocq/pulls/501[vidocq#501]).
* The Mansart Data panel of the dev console shows its boot facts only when the application has no Jakarta
  Transactions: `vidocq extension add mansart-transactions` gives it the query editor back.
* Running `vidocq test` while `vidocq dev` runs rewrites `target/vidocq-dev-services.properties` with the test
  session's database, which the next dev startup report then shows. The application keeps its own database;
  restart `vidocq dev` to see its coordinates again.

== Try it

The CLI is a Java 25 application with a launcher for each shell, so it runs on macOS, Linux and Windows. 0.4.0-RC2 is
the latest release on Maven Central, so the installer picks it up:

[source,bash]
----
curl -fsSL https://vidocq.dev/install.sh | sh
vidocq version        # Vidocq CLI 0.4.0-RC2
vidocq create --name my-app -x cassini-rest
cd my-app
vidocq dev
----

On Windows, in PowerShell:

[source,powershell]
----
irm https://vidocq.dev/install.ps1 | iex
----

A CLI you already have says `vidocq update`. The
https://doc.vidocq.dev/vidocq/0.4.0-RC2/getting-started[getting-started guide] and the
https://doc.vidocq.dev/tutorials/0.4.0-RC2/rest-and-database[REST and database tutorial] are on the 0.4.0-RC2 line of
the documentation. Write a resource, run it, point your favourite load generator at it, and tell us what you measure
on your own machine. If something does not work, tell us on https://codefloe.com/Vidocq/vidocq/issues[CodeFloe]: a
release candidate is the moment when a report costs us the least.

Next comes `0.4.0`: MicroProfile 7.2 on its final artifacts, and the security baseline of the

== How we measured

* *What was measured.* The 0.4.0-SNAPSHOT of Vidocq's `main` branch, as deployed on 2026-10-28 for the start-up and
memory figures, and on 2026-11-03 for the load figures; each jar is identified by its checksum. The 0.4.0-RC2 release
is cut from that line, and the release build itself has not been measured yet.
* *Two suites.* Start-up and memory come from a suite without JFR or GC logging, on Temurin 25.0.4.1. Load comes from
a suite on Temurin 25.0.5 that records JFR and GC logs, and reports a slower start and more memory at rest (1.20 s,
170 MB) for that reason.
* *One machine, one run per rate.* Each rate of the load suite ran once, so differences of a few percent are noise.
The start-up figures are medians of 7 cold starts.
* *CRaC.* The restore figures come from the
link:/posts/checkpoint-restore-answer-vidocq-on-crac/[CRaC post]: same machine, Azul Zulu 25.0.4.1 CRaC, Vidocq
built from `main` before the application events, medians of 3 to 7 runs per tier.
* *The CLI session* ran once, on a Mac with an Apple M5 Max and Docker through OrbStack; its timings are what the
tools printed that day, not a benchmark.
